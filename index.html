Key Observation
📊 DEMOGRAPHIC INSIGHT
Gender Parity is Strong; Profile Gap is Narrow
Female mentors outperform males marginally (72.1% vs 71.2%), and show lower Red rates (6.8% vs 10.4%), suggesting gender is not a risk factor. Co-Mentors lead at 75.9% — likely due to their support role and prior exposure — while Old and New Mentors are statistically equivalent (71.2% vs 71.3%), suggesting that experience alone is not translating into superior performance.
Strengths Identified
STRENGTH Teaching Practice & Facilitation
  • 🎯
    Session Flow & Structure
    Many mentors demonstrated clear lesson structure with well-paced introductions, logical flow, and strong reflections. Evaluators noted "seamless session flow" and "clear objective delivery."
  • 🤝
    Learner Engagement & Participation
    High engagement through energizers, group activities, and checking for understanding. Noted strength particularly in Mayuge cohort — "engaged everyone in class."
  • 💪
    Confidence & Presence
    Mentors across all sites showed strong personal presence. Frequent comments: "confident," "passionate," "audible," and "time-conscious."
STRENGTH Culture & Professionalism
  • ⏰
    Time Management & Punctuality
    Strong time management cited across sites — "use of time reminders during group activities," and intentional pacing throughout lessons.
  • 📋
    Visual Aids & Classroom Prep
    "Well-written flipchart," clear visual materials, and structured props noted as highlights. Mentors came prepared with relevant teaching tools.
  • 🌱
    Peer Support & Growth Mindset
    Multiple mentors noted for supporting struggling peers, continuous feedback-seeking, and demonstrating eagerness to learn and improve.
Development Areas
GAP Knowledge Mastery
  • 📚
    Weak Content Mastery
    52% of mentors scored Red on Knowledge Share. Common feedback: "struggling with content mastery," conceptual confusion on key topics (e.g., PEDVU framework), and inability to simplify complex ideas for learners.
  • ❓
    Lack of Checking for Understanding
    Across all sites, evaluators consistently flagged that mentors "explain concepts clearly but don't check for learners' understanding." This is the single most recurring development theme.
GAP Lesson Mock Performance
  • 🎭
    Classroom Management Gaps
    61% scored Red on Lesson Mock. Feedback highlights: unclear instructions, poor management of group activities, "awkward silence" moments, and failure to use ISTUCK techniques.
  • 🔄
    Delivery Consistency & Movement
    Evaluators noted "no intentional movement," "too much book nursing," and mentors not linking visual aids to lesson content. Energy levels and delivery pacing need significant work.

What's Working Well
✅ CURRENT WIN
Attendance is a Cohort Strength
74% of all mentors scored Green on Attendance (full 10/10 days). Only 8% Red. All three sites show attendance above 90%, led by Kampala at 9.9/10 average. This signals strong motivation and commitment across the cohort.
✅ CURRENT WIN
Mayuge is a Model Site — 54% Green Overall
Mayuge leads all sites by a significant margin with 81.1% average score. 54% (45 out of 83) achieved Green rating overall. Knowledge Share (14.0/18), Lesson Mock (52.8/75) and Culture (29.3/36) all significantly outperform other sites. Mayuge's practices should be documented and shared as best-practice benchmarks.
✅ CURRENT WIN
Co-Mentors & Female Mentors Lead in Performance
Co-Mentors average 75.9% — 4–5 percentage points ahead of Old and New Mentor cohorts. Female mentors score marginally higher (72.1% vs 71.2%) and have lower Red rates (6.8% vs 10.4%). The support and co-teaching model appears effective.
✅ CURRENT WIN
New Mentors Equal Old Mentors — Promising Pipeline
New Mentors (71.3%) statistically match Old Mentors (71.2%) — a promising sign that the induction programme is onboarding talent effectively. The 2026 cohort appears well-prepared to step into mentor roles without significant experience disadvantage.
What Requires Attention
🚨 CRITICAL GAP
Lesson Mock: 61% Red — The Biggest Risk to Youth Outcomes
Only 8% of mentors scored Green on Lesson Mock (≥60/75). This is the weakest component across all sites and all demographic segments. If left unaddressed, this directly threatens the quality of learning for the 36,000+ youth these mentors serve.
  • Mbarara avg: 38.9/75 vs. Mayuge's 52.8 — a 14-point gap
  • Failure to check for understanding is the #1 recurring gap
  • ISTUCK framework not being applied consistently
🚨 CRITICAL GAP
Mbarara: No Green Mentors Overall — Systemic Issue
Mbarara is the only site with zero Green-rated mentors in the overall assessment — despite 125 mentors assessed (largest cohort). With 24 Red-rated mentors and lowest scores on both Mock (38.9) and Culture (22.9), this site needs a dedicated intervention plan before school deployment.
⚡ MONITOR
Knowledge Share: 52% Red Cohort-Wide
Only 11% (34 mentors) scored Green on Knowledge Share. Content mastery and ability to simplify concepts are consistently flagged. This isn't site-specific — it's a curriculum or pre-induction preparation gap that needs addressing programme-wide.
  • Kampala avg: 10.7/18 — just above the Red threshold
  • Mbarara avg: 11.3/18 — borderline
  • Only Mayuge reaches above-amber zone (14.0/18)
⚡ MONITOR
Culture & Professionalism: Kampala and Mbarara Below Target
Both Kampala (23.7/36) and Mbarara (22.9/36) fall below the Green threshold of 30. Only Mayuge approaches target levels (29.3/36). Culture scores reflect a broader gap in Educate! way-of-work adoption, particularly classroom management standards.
Recommended Actions
1
Deploy Intensive Remediation for Mbarara Red-Rated Mentors
The 24 Red-rated mentors in Mbarara must receive individualized coaching plans before Term 1 school deployment. Assign experienced Co-Mentors from Mayuge as peer coaches. Focus on Lesson Mock practice using ISTUCK framework and structured observation cycles.
🚨 IMMEDIATE — Before School Deployment
2
Address Lesson Mock Weakness Cohort-Wide Through Structured Practice Sessions
Only 8% achieved Green on Lesson Mock. Introduce weekly 30-min peer observation and feedback loops during Term 1. Create a shared micro-coaching toolkit that emphasizes checking for understanding, intentional movement, and classroom management.
🚨 IMMEDIATE — Week 1 of Term 1
3
Investigate and Document Mayuge's Success Formula
Mayuge's 81.1% average and 54% Green rate is exceptional. Commission a brief qualitative study of what's driving these outcomes — trainer quality, site culture, preparation approaches. Document and share as a replication playbook for Kampala and Mbarara for T2.
⚡ SHORT-TERM — By End of Term 1
4
Strengthen Pre-Induction Content Preparation Across All Sites
52% Red on Knowledge Share is a programme-design gap, not just execution. Review whether pre-induction materials and self-study protocols are adequate. Consider a pre-assessment test 2 weeks before the next induction to identify at-risk mentors early.
⚡ SHORT-TERM — T2 Induction Design
5
Celebrate and Leverage High-Performers as Peer Champions
The 52 Green-rated mentors — concentrated in Mayuge — are an under-utilised asset. Formally recognise their performance, and deploy them as peer mentors and model practitioners across their sites to build a culture of excellence within the cohort.
✅ SUSTAIN — Ongoing
